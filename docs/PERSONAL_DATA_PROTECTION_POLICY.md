## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose
This Personal Data Protection Policy establishes the framework for protecting personal data processed by Andiza Co. Limited in compliance with Rwanda Law No 058/2021 on the Protection of Personal Data and Privacy.

### 1.2 Scope
This policy applies to:
- All employees, contractors, and third parties processing personal data on behalf of Andiza
- All personal data collected, processed, stored, or transmitted by Andiza
- All systems, applications, and infrastructure used to process personal data
- All business units and departments within Andiza

### 1.3 Objectives
- Ensure lawful, fair, and transparent processing of personal data
- Protect the rights and freedoms of data subjects
- Minimize risks associated with data processing
- Establish accountability and governance
- Comply with Rwanda Law No 058/2021 and international best practices

## 3. Data Protection Principles

In accordance with Rwanda Law No 058/2021, we adhere to the following principles:

### 3.1 Lawfulness, Fairness, and Transparency
- Process data lawfully based on valid legal grounds
- Be transparent about data processing activities
- Provide clear privacy notices to data subjects

### 3.2 Purpose Limitation
- Collect data for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes
- Not process data in ways incompatible with original purposes
- Obtain new consent for new purposes

### 3.3 Data Minimization
- Collect only data adequate, relevant, and necessary
- Avoid excessive data collection
- Regularly review data collection practices

### 3.4 Accuracy
- Ensure personal data is accurate and up-to-date
- Take reasonable steps to correct inaccurate data
- Enable data subjects to update their information

### 3.5 Storage Limitation
- Retain data only as long as necessary
- Implement data retention schedules
- Securely delete or anonymize data after retention period

### 3.6 Integrity and Confidentiality
- Implement appropriate security measures
- Protect against unauthorized access, loss, or damage
- Ensure confidentiality of personal data

### 3.7 Accountability
- Demonstrate compliance with data protection principles
- Maintain records of processing activities
- Conduct regular audits and assessments

## 5. Legal Bases for Processing

We process personal data based on the following legal grounds:

### 5.1 Consent (Article 6)
- Freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous
- Obtained before processing begins
- Documented and verifiable
- Withdrawable at any time

**Examples**:
- Marketing communications
- Optional features and services
- Cookies (non-essential)

### 5.2 Contract Performance (Article 7)
- Necessary for contract execution
- Pre-contractual measures at data subject's request

**Examples**:
- Account creation
- Service delivery
- Payment processing

### 5.3 Legal Obligation (Article 8)
- Required by law or regulation

**Examples**:
- Tax records
- Financial reporting
- Court orders

### 5.4 Legitimate Interest (Article 9)
- Necessary for legitimate interests
- Not overridden by data subject's rights
- Documented and justified

**Examples**:
- Fraud prevention
- Network security
- Internal analytics

## 6. Data Subject Rights

### 6.1 Right of Access (Article 52)
**Process**:
- Verify identity of requester
- Provide copy of personal data within 30 days
- Include information about processing
- Free of charge (first request)

### 6.2 Right to Rectification (Article 53)
**Process**:
- Verify identity and accuracy of correction
- Update data within 30 days
- Notify third parties if applicable

### 6.3 Right to Erasure (Article 54)
**Process**:
- Assess legal grounds for erasure
- Delete data within 30 days if applicable
- Notify third parties if applicable
- Document reasons for refusal if not granted

### 6.4 Right to Restriction (Article 55)
**Process**:
- Assess grounds for restriction
- Mark data as restricted
- Notify data subject before lifting restriction

### 6.5 Right to Data Portability (Article 56)
**Process**:
- Provide data in structured, machine-readable format
- Transfer directly to another controller if feasible
- Within 30 days

### 6.6 Right to Object (Article 57)
**Process**:
- Assess grounds for objection
- Stop processing unless compelling legitimate grounds
- Always honor objection to marketing

### 6.7 Response Procedures
- Acknowledge request within 5 business days
- Respond substantively within 30 days
- Extend by 60 days if complex (with explanation)
- Provide reasons if request is denied
- Inform of right to complain to authority

## 8. Data Breach Management

### 8.1 Breach Detection
- 24/7 monitoring systems
- Employee reporting channels
- Vendor notification requirements
- Regular security assessments

### 8.2 Breach Response (Articles 43-45)

**Immediate Actions (0-24 hours)**:
- Contain the breach
- Assess scope and impact
- Notify DPO and management
- Preserve evidence

**Authority Notification (Within 72 hours)**:
- Notify National Cyber Security Authority
- Provide breach details:
  - Nature of breach
  - Categories and number of data subjects affected
  - Likely consequences
  - Measures taken or proposed

**Data Subject Notification (Without undue delay)**:
- Notify affected individuals if high risk
- Provide clear information about:
  - Nature of breach
  - Contact point for information
  - Likely consequences
  - Measures taken or recommended

**Post-Breach Actions**:
- Conduct root cause analysis
- Implement corrective measures
- Update security measures
- Document lessons learned
- Update breach register

### 8.3 Breach Register
Maintained by DPO, including:
- Date and time of breach
- Nature and scope of breach
- Data subjects affected
- Consequences and impact
- Remedial actions taken
- Authority and data subject notifications

## 9. Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)

### 9.1 When DPIA is Required (Article 38)
- Large-scale processing of sensitive data
- Systematic monitoring of public areas
- Automated decision-making with legal effects
- Processing of biometric or genetic data
- New technologies with high privacy risks

### 9.2 DPIA Process
1. **Describe processing**: Purpose, scope, data flows
2. **Assess necessity**: Proportionality and compliance
3. **Identify risks**: Privacy and security risks
4. **Assess risks**: Likelihood and severity
5. **Identify measures**: Mitigating controls
6. **Document**: DPIA report
7. **Review**: DPO and management approval
8. **Consult**: Authority if high residual risk

### 9.3 DPIA Review
- Before implementing new processing
- When significant changes occur
- Periodically (at least every 2 years)
