## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose
This procedure establishes the framework for responding to and notifying relevant parties of personal data breaches in compliance with Articles 43-45 of Rwanda Law No 058/2021 on the Protection of Personal Data and Privacy.

### 1.2 Scope
This procedure applies to:
- All personal data breaches affecting Andiza Co. Limited
- All employees, contractors, and third-party processors
- All systems, applications, and infrastructure
- All types of breaches (confidentiality, integrity, availability)

### 1.3 Objectives
- Detect and contain breaches quickly
- Assess breach severity and impact
- Notify the Supervisory Authority within 72 hours
- Notify affected data subjects without undue delay
- Document all breaches in the breach register
- Implement corrective measures to prevent recurrence

## 2. Legal Framework

### 2.1 Rwanda Law No 058/2021

**Article 43**: Notification to Supervisory Authority within 72 hours  
**Article 44**: Notification to data subjects without undue delay if high risk  
**Article 45**: Documentation and record-keeping requirements

### 2.2 Penalties for Non-Compliance
- Administrative fines
- Criminal penalties
- Reputational damage
- Civil liability to data subjects

## 3. What Constitutes a Data Breach

### 3.1 Definition
A breach of security leading to accidental or unlawful:
- Destruction of personal data
- Loss of personal data
- Alteration of personal data
- Unauthorized disclosure of personal data
- Unauthorized access to personal data

### 3.2 Types of Breaches

**Confidentiality Breach**:
- Unauthorized access to personal data
- Unauthorized disclosure or sharing
- Data sent to wrong recipient
- Lost or stolen devices containing data
- Hacking or cyber attack

**Integrity Breach**:
- Unauthorized alteration of personal data
- Data corruption
- Ransomware attack
- Malicious modification

**Availability Breach**:
- Loss of access to personal data
- Accidental deletion
- System failure
- Denial of service attack
- Natural disaster affecting data

### 3.3 Examples of Data Breaches

**YES - These are breaches**:
- Email containing personal data sent to wrong recipient
- Laptop stolen containing unencrypted personal data
- Hacker gains access to user database
- Ransomware encrypts customer data
- Employee accesses data without authorization
- Cloud storage misconfiguration exposes data publicly
- Phishing attack compromises admin credentials
- USB drive with personal data lost
- Paper records left in public place
- Unauthorized disclosure to third party

**NO - These are NOT breaches** (but may require action):
- Authorized access to data for legitimate purpose
- Data loss prevented by backup recovery
- Attempted breach that was blocked
- Encrypted data stolen (if encryption is strong and keys secure)
- Anonymized data disclosed (if truly anonymized)

## 6. PHASE 1: Detection and Reporting (0-1 hour)

### 6.1 Detection Methods

**Automated Detection**:
- Security monitoring alerts
- Intrusion detection systems
- Log analysis tools
- Anomaly detection
- Data loss prevention (DLP) tools

**Manual Detection**:
- Employee reports
- Customer complaints
- Third-party notifications
- Audit findings
- Media reports

### 6.2 Reporting Channels

**Internal Reporting**:
- Email: security@andiza.co.rw
- Phone: [Security Hotline]
- Incident reporting form: [Internal URL]
- Direct contact to DPO or IT Security

**External Reporting**:
- Third-party processors must notify within 24 hours
- Vendors and partners: [Contact details]

### 6.3 Initial Report

Anyone discovering a potential breach must immediately report:
- Date and time of discovery
- Nature of the incident
- Systems or data affected
- How the breach was discovered
- Any immediate actions taken
- Contact details of reporter

**DO NOT**:
- Delay reporting to investigate further
- Attempt to fix the issue without reporting
- Discuss the breach publicly or on social media
- Delete evidence or logs

### 6.4 Immediate Actions (Within 1 hour)

1. **Alert the DPO** immediately
2. **Preserve evidence**: Do not delete logs, emails, or files
3. **Document everything**: Start incident log
4. **Isolate affected systems** (if safe to do so)
5. **Assemble response team**

## 7. PHASE 2: Initial Assessment (1-4 hours)

### 7.1 Breach Assessment Meeting

**Convened by**: DPO  
**Attendees**: Incident Response Team  
**Timeline**: Within 2 hours of detection

### 7.2 Assessment Questions

**What happened?**
- Type of breach (confidentiality, integrity, availability)
- How did the breach occur?
- When did the breach occur?
- When was it discovered?

**What data is affected?**
- Categories of personal data
- Volume of data
- Sensitivity of data
- Special categories (if any)

**Who is affected?**
- Categories of data subjects
- Number of individuals
- Vulnerable groups (children, etc.)

**What are the consequences?**
- Potential harm to data subjects
- Likelihood of harm
- Severity of harm

**Is it contained?**
- Is the breach ongoing?
- Can it be contained?
- What containment measures are needed?

### 7.3 Severity Classification

**CRITICAL** (Red):
- Large-scale breach (>10,000 individuals)
- Special categories of data (health, biometric, financial)
- High risk of identity theft or fraud
- Unencrypted sensitive data
- Ongoing breach with no containment

**HIGH** (Orange):
- Medium-scale breach (1,000-10,000 individuals)
- Financial data or payment information
- Significant risk of harm
- Breach contained but data exposed
- Reputational damage likely

**MEDIUM** (Yellow):
- Small-scale breach (100-1,000 individuals)
- Limited sensitive data
- Low to moderate risk of harm
- Breach contained quickly
- Minimal reputational impact

**LOW** (Green):
- Very small-scale (<100 individuals)
- Non-sensitive data only
- Minimal risk of harm
- Breach contained immediately
- No reputational impact

### 7.4 Notification Decision

**Supervisory Authority Notification Required?**
- YES: All breaches (unless unlikely to result in risk)
- Timeline: Within 72 hours

**Data Subject Notification Required?**
- YES: If high risk to rights and freedoms
- Timeline: Without undue delay

**Factors for High Risk**:
- Large number of individuals affected
- Sensitive or special categories of data
- Vulnerable data subjects (children)
- Potential for identity theft or fraud
- Potential for financial loss
- Potential for reputational damage
- Potential for discrimination or disadvantage

## 8. PHASE 3: Containment (4-24 hours)

### 8.1 Immediate Containment Actions

**Technical Containment**:
- Isolate affected systems from network
- Disable compromised user accounts
- Change passwords and access credentials
- Block malicious IP addresses
- Patch vulnerabilities
- Restore from clean backups (if needed)

**Physical Containment**:
- Secure physical premises
- Retrieve lost or stolen devices
- Secure paper records
- Restrict physical access

**Administrative Containment**:
- Suspend affected services (if necessary)
- Notify third-party processors
- Engage external experts (if needed)
- Preserve evidence for investigation

### 8.2 Evidence Preservation

**Digital Evidence**:
- Preserve system logs
- Take forensic images of affected systems
- Preserve email communications
- Document system configurations
- Capture screenshots

**Physical Evidence**:
- Secure physical documents
- Preserve access logs
- Retain CCTV footage
- Document physical security status

### 8.3 Containment Verification

Confirm:
- Breach is fully contained
- No ongoing unauthorized access
- Vulnerabilities are patched
- Systems are secure
- Evidence is preserved

---

## 9. PHASE 4: Investigation (24-72 hours)

### 9.1 Root Cause Analysis

Investigate:
- How did the breach occur?
- What vulnerabilities were exploited?
- Were security measures adequate?
- Were policies and procedures followed?
- Could the breach have been prevented?

### 9.2 Impact Assessment

Determine:
- Exact data compromised
- Exact number of individuals affected
- Likelihood and severity of harm
- Potential consequences for data subjects
- Reputational and financial impact

### 9.3 Investigation Methods

- Log analysis
- Forensic examination
- Interviews with personnel
- Review of security measures
- Third-party forensic investigation (if needed)

### 9.4 Investigation Documentation

Document:
- Timeline of events
- Systems and data affected
- Individuals affected
- Root cause
- Contributing factors
- Evidence collected
- Findings and conclusions

## 10. PHASE 5: Notification (Within 72 hours)

### 10.1 Notification to Supervisory Authority (Article 43)

**Timeline**: Within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach

**Authority**: National Cyber Security Authority

**Method**: [Specify: Email, online portal, registered mail]

**Contact**:
- Email: [Authority Email]
- Phone: [Authority Phone]
- Address: [Authority Address]

**Information to Provide** (minimum):

1. **Nature of the breach**:
   - Description of the incident
   - Type of breach
   - Date and time of breach
   - Date and time of discovery

2. **Categories and number of data subjects**:
   - Types of individuals affected
   - Approximate number

3. **Categories and number of records**:
   - Types of data affected
   - Approximate volume

4. **Likely consequences**:
   - Potential harm to data subjects
   - Risk assessment

5. **Measures taken or proposed**:
   - Containment actions
   - Remediation measures
   - Preventive measures

6. **Contact point**:
   - DPO name and contact details

**If information not available within 72 hours**:
- Notify with available information
- Provide reasons for delay
- Provide timeline for additional information
- Submit supplementary notification when available

**Use Form**: Data Breach Notification Form to Supervisory Authority (separate document)

### 10.2 Notification to Data Subjects (Article 44)

**Timeline**: Without undue delay (if high risk)

**Method**: Direct communication (email, letter, SMS, phone)

**Language**: Clear and plain language

**Information to Provide**:

1. **Nature of the breach**:
   - What happened (in simple terms)
   - What data was affected

2. **Contact point**:
   - DPO or privacy team contact details

3. **Likely consequences**:
   - What it means for the individual
   - Potential risks

4. **Measures taken or proposed**:
   - What we've done to address the breach
   - What we're doing to prevent recurrence

5. **Recommendations**:
   - Steps individuals can take to protect themselves
   - Resources and support available

**Exceptions** (notification not required if):
- Appropriate technical protection (e.g., encryption)
- Subsequent measures ensure no high risk
- Disproportionate effort (then public communication)

**Use Form**: Data Breach Notification Form to Data Subjects (separate document)

### 10.3 Public Communication

**When Required**:
- Disproportionate effort to contact individuals directly
- High public interest
- Regulatory requirement
- Media attention

**Method**:
- Website notice
- Press release
- Social media
- Public advertisement

**Approval**: CEO and DPO

### 11.1 Immediate Remediation

- Update policies and procedures
- Provide additional training
- Implement monitoring

### 11.2 Support for Data Subjects

- Respond to inquiries promptly
- Provide regular updates

### 11.3 Regulatory Cooperation

- Provide additional information as needed
